# Build your own GridDividend explorer

GridDividend's results are published as one JSON file per utility in exports/
(regenerated by scripts/export_results.py). You can ask an AI assistant that writes
and runs code to build an interactive explorer from them. Give it the export files
(or a link to this repository), docs/GLOSSARY.md, and the prompt below.

## The prompt

Build an interactive explorer for the GridDividend results in exports/<utility>.json
(ConEd, RGE, DomVA, PepcoMD, EversourceCT, UI_CT).
Use only figures from those files: don't estimate, round away or invent any number.
Read docs/GLOSSARY.md for the terms, and each file's metadata notes (flows_note,
share_sweep_note, objective_sweep_note, objective_sweep_run_note, fast_start_note,
allocation_sweep_note, earnings_note, transmission_note, lookback_note, tornado_note)
for what each block means. Make it a single self-contained web page
that works at phone width and in light and dark themes, with tooltips on charts and
no meaning carried by colour alone.

The data, per utility file:
- metadata: names (utility, name, short_name), source_commit,
  source_modified_since_commit, generated date, dollar_basis, pool_setting,
  bau_basis, residential_sales_share, scenarios, paces, years.
- runs[pace][scenario]: pace is utility_forecast or state_policy; scenario is
  Cautious, Central or Ambitious. Each run has:
  - yearly: average residential bill by category for business as usual and shared
    savings (avg_residential_bill_usd_month.bau / .shared_savings: distribution_base,
    distribution_rider, program_surcharge, supply_energy, capacity, transmission,
    taxes_and_fees, total), customer_savings_m, rate_base_m, equity_return_m,
    shared_savings_earnings_m, earnings_total_m, earnings_per_rate_base, and,
    reported separately, lower_share_of_zonal_transmission_costs_m and
    cost_shifted_to_other_zone_customers_m;
  - waterfall_usd_month: each layer's effect on the 2035 and 2050 bill, from BAU
    to Shared savings;
  - value_flows_m: money-flow sources, program_costs, net_value, to and links,
    undiscounted_nominal and npv3_2026;
  - headline: customer_savings_b_undiscounted, customer_savings_b_npv3,
    shared_savings_earnings_b, monthly_saving_2035, monthly_saving_2050,
    bau_bill_2050, and, reported separately,
    lower_share_of_zonal_transmission_costs_b and cost_shifted_to_other_zone_customers_b;
  - share_sweep: keys "0.20", "0.30", "0.40" (utility share);
  - objective_sweep: keys "0.000", "0.025", "0.050", "0.100" (additional savings
    objective), each with a full run block under run;
  - fast_start: a full run (yearly, waterfall_usd_month, value_flows_m, headline)
    with savings starting in 2027;
  - allocation_sweep: by_sales, per_customer, custom (custom = half and half,
    residential share 0.5), each with yearly, value_flows_m and headline
    (residential and non-residential savings and bill savings);
  - load_calibration: how business-as-usual load was matched to the utility's
    forecast (background only).
- tornado[pace]: central_saving_usd_month and rows; each row is one input tested
  low and high at Central (low_2035, low_2050, high_2035, high_2050, swing_2050),
  with layer, assumption and type (policy or external).
- lookback: 2021–2026 bill trend (bill_trend), the 2026 tariff bill
  (bill_2026_tariff), the revenue-requirement bridge (rr_bridge: rows and
  grouped_rows), rate cases asked against granted (proceedings and
  asked_granted_totals), capacity prices (supply_side.capacity_prices: pjm, nyiso or
  isone, whichever is not null; ISO-NE prices are $/kW-month by capability period),
  Connecticut's Standard Service and public-benefits series
  (supply_side.standard_service_cents_kwh, supply_side.public_benefits_cents_kwh), class
  allocation (class_allocation), and Dominion's riders (riders, Dominion file only).
- Ignore the advance blocks (runs[...].advance and advance_fast_start, the top-level
  advance_stress and advance_fast_start_stress, and metadata.advance_note): the
  dividend advance is a parked option.

Build these views, in this order:
1. Controls: utility, scenario, electrification pace, when savings start (as planned
   or fast start), households' share (by sales, per customer, half and half) and
   additional savings objective. Only some combinations are exported: the objective
   exists only at planned timing and by sales; the households' share only at planned
   timing and with no objective; fast start only with no objective and by sales.
   When one is chosen, reset the others and say so.
2. Starting point, 2021–2026: the bill trend (delivery and supply where available),
   what drove the revenue requirement, what the utility asked for against what
   regulators approved, and capacity prices.
3. Headline figures: customer savings (with NPV 3% and the households' share),
   the 2035 and 2050 monthly residential saving, the share of the 2050
   business-as-usual bill saved, and shared savings earnings.
4. The monthly bill 2026–2050: business as usual against shared savings, with a
   band from Cautious to Ambitious.
5. The utility's earnings: return on rate base plus shared savings earnings against
   business as usual, and earnings per dollar of rate base.
6. Layer by layer: each layer's effect on the 2035 or 2050 bill.
7. Where the value goes: a money-flow (Sankey) diagram from each source, less
   program costs, to households, businesses and the utility's earnings.
8. What moves the result: the largest sensitivities, coloured policy or external.
9. The utility's share (20/30/40%) and the additional savings objective compared.
10. A glossary at the bottom, linked from the top.

Use plain names, not the model's internal ones:
system_needs = "Load growth (both cases)"; deferral = "Grid upgrades deferred";
programs = "Program costs (flexibility, efficiency)"; load_enabled = "New load
connected sooner"; capacity = "Lower capacity costs"; wholesale_energy = "Lower
wholesale energy prices"; transmission = "Transmission upgrades deferred"; losses =
"Less energy lost on the grid"; objective = "Additional savings objective";
mechanism = "Utility's share of the savings"; roe = "Change in allowed return";
rate-case step (lookback rr_bridge step_2026) = "Approved increase".

Rules for honesty:
- Scenarios are sets of assumptions, not forecasts or probabilities.
- Figures are nominal and undiscounted unless marked NPV 3%.
- Business as usual is the utility's own published plan.
- The additional savings objective is additional by assumption: present it as
  "if the utility finds a further X%".
- "Lower share of zonal transmission costs" (lower_share_of_zonal_transmission_costs_*)
  is a cost shift to other customers in the PJM zone (in Connecticut, the rest of New
  England). Show it separately with the
  cost shifted (cost_shifted_to_other_zone_customers_*); never count it as savings.
- Show the source commit and generation date, and say the page is a snapshot that
  must be rebuilt when the exports change.
